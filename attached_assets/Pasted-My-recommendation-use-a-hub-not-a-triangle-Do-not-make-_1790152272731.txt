My recommendation: use a hub, not a triangle
Do not make the bank account independently feed both the calendar and the chat, and do not make the calendar the middle layer.

Use this conceptual model:

Bank accounts, uploaded documents, and manual inputs → one financial data layer → calendar, goals, forecasts, and chat

The chat and calendar should be two views of the same underlying financial information. The chat should not maintain its own interpretation of the user’s finances, because that will eventually create contradictions:

The chat says the user can afford AED 2,000.
The calendar shows an upcoming school payment it did not know about.
The bank balance looks healthy, but a credit-card payment is due tomorrow.
The central data layer should know which information is actual, forecasted, recurring, pending, manually entered, or uncertain.

The most important feature to add: “safe to spend”
A balance display is not enough. The app should show:

Current available balance
Expected income before the next payday
Bills and commitments due before then
Minimum debt payments
Planned goal contributions
A recommended emergency buffer
Safe to spend until payday
Safe to spend this month
What changes if the user spends AED 500, AED 2,000, or AED 10,000
This should become the app’s main daily answer. Competitors such as YNAB focus on assigning money to priorities, while Monarch emphasizes flexible/category budgeting, goals, forecasting, and collaboration. Copilot focuses on automated tracking, spending, investments, and net worth. Your opportunity is to combine those ideas around future cash-flow visibility for UAE households, rather than simply copying a monthly budget screen. 
 
 

What the calendar should actually contain
The calendar should be a money timeline, not just a list of recurring payments.

Each item should show:

Expected date
Expected amount or amount range
Account it will come from
Payment type: rent, salary, card payment, tuition, insurance, subscription, loan, utility, etc.
Actual, forecasted, pending, or overdue status
Confidence level
Whether the amount is fixed or variable
Whether the user has reviewed it
The balance after the payment
The effect on a goal or emergency reserve
Important UAE-specific cases include:

Rent paid by cheque or several large instalments
Annual insurance payments
School fees and tuition instalments
Credit-card statement dates versus payment due dates
Salary dates that move around weekends or holidays
End-of-service payments
Remittances to family abroad
Visa, tenancy, vehicle registration, and renewal expenses
Variable utilities and service charges
Ramadan, Eid, travel, and other seasonal spending
The calendar should also detect recurring payments automatically, but put uncertain discoveries into a Review inbox. “Seamless” should not mean silently making decisions. It should mean the user reviews ten suggested items instead of manually entering fifty.

Add a proper goals and reserves system
The auto-set-aside idea is good, but make it more flexible than one savings bucket.

A user should be able to create:

Emergency fund
Home deposit
Car purchase
School or university fees
Wedding
Travel
Annual insurance
Business or freelance tax reserve
Family support
Major medical expense
Religious or charitable giving
Every goal needs:

Target amount
Target date
Priority
Current amount
Monthly contribution
Inflation assumption
Funding source
Whether the goal is essential, important, or optional
What happens if income falls
Initially, “set aside” can mean a virtual allocation in the plan. Moving real money automatically should come later and only through an appropriately regulated partner with explicit user approval.

The chat should be an action and explanation layer
The chat should not just answer generic financial questions. It should be able to work with the user’s actual plan:

“Can I afford this car?”
“Why is this month tighter than usual?”
“Move my holiday goal from December to March.”
“Add a school payment of AED 18,000 every September.”
“What bills are due before my next salary?”
“If I lose my income for three months, what happens?”
“Show me the cheapest way to reach my home deposit.”
“Why did my spending increase this month?”
“Which subscriptions have I not used recently?”
“Compare paying cash versus borrowing.”
For any action that changes the plan, the chat should show a preview and require confirmation. It should never quietly edit a recurring payment, move a goal, or initiate a transfer.

Every important answer should include:

The data it used
The date the data was last refreshed
Assumptions
What is known versus estimated
A range, not false precision
A clear next step
The assistant should also be allowed to say: “I cannot determine that from the information available.”

Make loan advice neutral and measurable
The brief says the product should help users feel comfortable taking loans. I would change that objective. The product should help people make responsible borrowing decisions, whether the answer is yes, no, or “wait.”

Otherwise users may eventually distrust the app, especially if banks or lenders are paying for referrals.

For every loan, calculate and display:

Monthly payment
Total amount repaid
Total interest or profit
Processing fees
Insurance and mandatory add-ons
Valuation, arrangement, or administrative fees
Early-settlement cost
Late-payment consequences
Salary-transfer requirements
Fixed versus variable rate
Effective annualized cost based on the full cash-flow schedule
Effect on emergency savings
Effect on the user’s existing goals
Worst projected month after taking the loan
What happens if income drops or the loan is delayed
There probably should not be one invented “official measure” presented as the answer. Use the figures required in the lender’s official disclosure, then add your own standard comparison called something like Total Cost of Borrowing. That should show the total AED cost and an annualized comparison calculated from the actual payment schedule.

For UAE localization, the Central Bank’s rules and consumer-protection material should be treated as primary sources, including its personal-loan and mortgage guidance. 
 
 

Mortgage versus rent needs a full scenario model
Do not reduce this to “rent versus monthly mortgage payment.” The comparison should include:

Down payment
Registration and transaction costs
Mortgage fees
Insurance
Maintenance
Service charges
Rent increases
Property price changes
Investment return on money used for the down payment
Opportunity cost
Expected years in the property
Exit or selling costs
Residency and job stability
Different appreciation and rent-inflation scenarios
For Dubai, the official Dubai Land Department rental index is a useful source for rental context, but it should be treated as market data—not as a personalized prediction.

Nationality should only be collected where it materially affects an eligibility or regulatory calculation, and the app should explain why it is asking. The user should be able to use the core budgeting features without handing over unnecessary sensitive details.

Documents

Useful file formats include PDF, CSV, XLSX, OFX/QFX, QIF, and images. The app should extract information, flag uncertainty, detect duplicates, and ask the user to confirm important fields.

UAE data sources for the knowledge system
Use a source-backed knowledge system rather than trying to “teach the AI everything.”

Good primary sources include:

Central Bank of the UAE: open finance, consumer protection, personal loans, mortgages, and financial-sector rules. 
UAE Federal Competitiveness and Statistics Centre / UAE.Stat: consumer-price data, inflation, and official statistics. 
Dubai Land Department: rental and property-market indices. 
Etihad Credit Bureau: official UAE credit score and credit-report information. 
Individual bank product documents: current rates, fees, eligibility, key facts, repayment schedules, and early-settlement terms.
Utility, education, healthcare, transport, and government fee authorities: used for specific estimates, always labeled by emirate and date.
There is no single official database containing “all UAE expenses.” The reliable approach is to combine the user’s real transactions with official inflation and public-cost data, then use carefully labeled estimates where official data does not exist.

Trust and safety are core features, not legal polish
Add these from the beginning:

Read-only bank permissions by default
Separate permissions for accounts, documents, and household members
Encryption and deletion controls
Clear data-retention settings
Audit history of every AI-generated recommendation and change
Source and date shown for regulatory claims
No guaranteed loan approval language
No hidden lender ranking
Clear separation between advice and sponsored offers
Human escalation for serious debt or financial distress
Strong warnings when the data is incomplete or stale
